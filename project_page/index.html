PhysPlan

Diagnosing Failure Bottlenecks in Latent World-Model Planning

A controlled framework for identifying whether recoverable planning performance is lost in prediction, decision metrics, or search before modifying the world model or planner.

?

A poor planning decision does not necessarily imply a poor world model.

Measure where decision quality is recoverable before deciding what to repair.

Core question

Where does a planning decision go wrong?

Prediction error is an obvious suspect when a planner chooses a poor action. But predicted futures can also be scored incorrectly, or search may never expose a good candidate. PhysPlan intervenes at one interface at a time and evaluates the selected action in the simulator.

Diagnose before repair

Turn diagnosis into a testable intervention choice

PhysPlan uses familiar oracle substitutions as controlled diagnostics. The methodological test is whether a frozen diagnosis corresponds to improvement at the targeted interface, with a pre-specified non-dominant-layer control where available.

The evidence strength differs by protocol: Local Push-T provides same-state intervention consistency, Wall provides prospective disjoint-state validation, and H6 is an adaptive-planner stress test.

Cross-regime view

The dominant recoverable bottleneck changes across planning regimes

Across the studied protocols, the ordering of prediction and decision-metric headroom changes. This migration is descriptive: the experiments do not isolate which individual factor caused the change.

Why it matters

Diagnosis should guide repair

Under these protocols, prediction error is not always the dominant recoverable source of planning failure.

Decision quality depends on multiple interfaces between a world model and a planner.

Controlled interventions can identify where recoverable decision headroom exists before expensive retraining.

Limitations and scope

A controlled study, not a universal diagnosis

The study is designed for auditable attribution. That control also bounds what the results establish.

  • Local Push-T and Wall use controlled candidate constructions; Wall uses a one-dimensional waypoint family.
  • The H6 study contains 30 episodes, and its scorer stress-test interval crosses zero.
  • Oracle gaps may interact and are not assumed additive.
  • The Wall predictor repair uses one training seed.
  • The results do not establish generality to policy learning, real robots, or arbitrary planners.
