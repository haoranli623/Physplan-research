\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{What the method adds.}
Oracle replacement is a familiar ablation device; PhysPlan's contribution is not the substitution in isolation. The added structure is candidate matching, simulator-grounded regret, frozen classification and repair rules, and targeted-versus-wrong-layer tests. It does not uniquely assign causal responsibility: gaps are practical ceilings under explicit substitutions, and components interact. The value is prospective localization before engineering effort, not an additive causal factorization.

\paragraph{Why not always improve prediction?}
Prediction is clearly limiting on Wall; in H6, both fixed-trace gaps are positive and the prediction point estimate satisfies the frozen prediction-important rule. It is not the dominant recoverable interface in controlled local Push-T, where a simple metric repair recovers substantial regret and higher predictor inference precision does not. The conclusion is therefore neither ``prediction does not matter'' nor ``metrics are generally limiting,'' but that the diagnosis must be repeated when the task, action family, or planner changes.

\paragraph{Scope.}
Controlled local Push-T uses a restricted local action slice, and Wall uses a controlled 1-D waypoint construction. These designs strengthen candidate-matched attribution but limit behavioral breadth. H6 supplies a realistic high-dimensional planning test, yet contains only 30 episodes; the scorer-test CI narrowly crosses zero, no paired CI compares $\Gpred$ directly with $\Gmetric$, and no H6 predictor repair tests the diagnosis-selected interface. Wall's predictor repair has one training seed, so its state-bootstrap CI is conditional on one learned model. The local Push-T repair shares final states and the readout with diagnosis, although its intervention was committed before computation; only Wall uses a disjoint repair set.

\paragraph{Oracle and interaction limits.}
The GT-readout condition combines representation observability with the finite capacity and development distribution of the ridge readout; it is a practical ceiling, not a pure representation oracle. Oracle gaps need not be additive. Under CEM, score, elite choice, and proposal coverage interact, so fixed-trace metric headroom is not equivalent to an end-to-end scorer intervention. The search interface is diagnosed but no search-dominant regime and corresponding repair are validated. Cross-task gap magnitudes use task-specific finite-set normalization and candidate families, so they are not a common physical scale.

\paragraph{Generality and reliability.}
The study covers one latent world-model family and two simulated tasks. ``Disjoint-state Wall validation'' means prospective evaluation on separate states within this project, not independent reproduction by another group. Differences across protocols descriptively establish changed headroom ordering, but confounded task and planner changes prevent a causal explanation of that migration. The study does not establish broad validity for real robots, vision--language--action models, learned policies, or other planners. Average gains conceal heterogeneous states: both controlled repairs harm some cases, and lower latent MSE does not guarantee state-wise decision improvement. Deployment would require uncertainty-aware gating or fallback mechanisms, which are outside this frozen study.
